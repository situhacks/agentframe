# The offer arc

Loaded at step 12, once, when an offer is live. Owns the whole offer stage: verify the package, price
it, build the ask, route it to the person who can grant it. The artifact it produces is `comp-case.md`
per [its template](deliverables/comp-case/template.md), which holds the shape; the judgment lives here.

Run this before the offer call. Once the answer is yes, every question below becomes a request for a
favour.

## 0. Request the documents before answering

**"Full health, dental and extended benefits" is not a disclosure.** Ask for the artifacts, in one
message, and expect them same-day:

- The **group benefits summary** — the carrier's plan-at-a-glance
- The **retirement plan details** — match formula, when eligibility starts, vesting
- The **full offer letter with every attachment**, including the equity plan and grant agreement
- The **leave policy** if the letter states only a number

Then take 24 to 48 hours. **The decision deadline is negotiable and asking usually costs nothing** — a
candidate weighing a competing process has extended a deadline by a week simply by asking the recruiter.

## 1. Verify the package

Base is one line of a dozen. Work every row; a blank is a question, not a zero.

**Cash.** Base. Bonus: target percent, formula or discretion, **and the eligibility calendar** — a 10%
target is worth nothing if the first payout lands sixteen months out under a performance cycle you
haven't joined yet. Ask when the first bonus and the first salary review actually land. Proration in
year one. Whether an active-employment clause voids it if you leave before payout. Signing bonus: the
clawback window, the triggers, and **whether repayment is gross or net** — a gross clawback means
repaying more than was received.

**Relocation.** Allowance or reimbursement against receipts, lump sum or capped, whether it is grossed
up for tax, temporary housing, shipping, a house-hunting trip, and the clawback if you leave early.
**Frequently never raised by either side**, which makes it the most available ask on the list when a
move is genuinely part of the deal.

**Equity.** Instrument, quantity, strike or grant price, current valuation and its date, vest schedule
and cliff, what happens at a liquidity event, the **post-termination exercise window** where option
value quietly dies, and whether refresh grants exist or the initial grant is the whole story. Separately,
whether an **employee stock purchase plan** exists — it is a different instrument from a grant, with its
own discount and enrolment window.

**Retirement.** The plan type first, because they behave differently on portability and vesting: group
RRSP, deferred profit sharing, defined contribution, defined benefit. Then the match formula stated as a
**percent of salary** so it can be benchmarked — a flat dollar cap looks generous until divided by the
base. Then four things people miss: whether the match **scales with level**, **when eligibility starts**
(a match beginning in eighteen months is not this year's compensation), whether employer contributions
vest, and whether the plan **consumes personal contribution room** you were going to use anyway.

**Health and dental.** **Who pays the premium** — employer-paid in full, or a payroll deduction that
makes this a monthly cost, and what the family tier costs if it applies. Then coinsurance and annual
maximum on drugs. Dental maximum, basic versus major split, orthodontics. Paramedical: per-practitioner
or one shared pool. Vision. Carrier. **Start date — day one or after a waiting period.**

**Spending accounts.** Health spending account, wellness account, flex credits, and their annual
declaration rules. **Professional development and certification budget.** Home office or equipment
allowance. Phone and internet stipend. Individually small, collectively worth thousands, and almost
never mentioned unless asked.

**Disability, life and illness.** Long-term disability percentage, monthly cap, **elimination period**,
and whether the benefit is taxable — an employee-paid premium usually means tax-free benefits, which is
the better design. Whether short-term disability bridges the elimination period or state benefits are
expected to. Life as a multiple of salary. Critical illness and AD&D. Out-of-country travel medical.

**Time.** Days, and whether vacation, sick and personal sit in **one combined bucket or separate ones** —
a 25-day combined bucket is not 25 vacation days. Whether it **scales with level**. Firm closure days on
top. Accrual versus front-load, carryover, payout on exit. Parental leave top-up.

**Work terms.** Required in-office days and whether that is contractual or policy. Travel expectation.
Probation length. **Termination terms** — statutory minimum only, or common-law notice preserved.
Non-compete, non-solicit, IP assignment.

**Progression.** Level and band, not just title. When the first review lands. The promotion cycle. What
changes at the next level, since match, vacation and bonus often move with it.

**Conditions.** What the offer is contingent on and what can withdraw it.

## 2. Split what is negotiable from what is fixed

**Individually negotiable:** base, signing bonus, relocation, equity grant, vacation, title and level,
start date, decision deadline.

**Not:** the group benefits plan, the retirement match formula, the disability schedule. Carrier
contracts and plan rules covering every employee. Nobody gets a personal drug coinsurance.

That split decides where the call goes. **A below-market benefit is not something to fix. It is an
argument for cash** — and only before you accept.

## 3. Where the gap actually gets closed

Base sits inside a band the recruiter often cannot exceed, especially after a level exception has
already been spent. **Signing and relocation come from discretionary budgets and move far more easily.**
So when base lands below target, ask for the gap there rather than reopening the number:

> *"The bonus calendar means year one is base-only, and I'm relocating across the country in the same
> window. Would there be room for a signing bonus or relocation support to bridge that? I'm not looking
> to reopen base."*

Two rules on delivery. **Route money asks to the recruiter, not the hiring manager** — a practice lead
cannot grant a signing budget and putting them on the thread only adds an audience. And **do not counter
your own number**: if you named a figure and received exactly it, reopening base is a credibility
problem. Point at the instruments that were never discussed instead.

## 4. Calibrate

Ranges move, so verify against current sources. As orientation for someone who has never seen a package
priced:

| | Typical |
|---|---|
| Employer retirement match | 3–5% of salary, starting on hire |
| Life insurance | 1–2× salary |
| Long-term disability | ~66.67% of earnings, monthly cap, 90–120 day elimination |
| Drug coinsurance | 80% standard; 100% is a premium plan |
| Paramedical | per-practitioner limits more common than one shared pool |
| Benefits start | day one is common; a waiting period is a real cost |
| Vacation | statutory minimum is a floor, not a market rate |

**One light item inside an otherwise average plan is normal. The failure is not knowing which item it
was** — and a strong non-cash package can legitimately offset a lower base, which you can only see once
every row above is filled in.

## 5. Write the case, then answer

`comp-case.md` per its template, with the verified package as its input: the reasons ranked by what
survives challenge, the script, and the objection table. Then `af pipe stage <slug> accepted` or
`declined`.

## The failure this arc exists to stop

Accepting a base in the belief that everything else is competitive, because nobody asked. It is the
most common mistake a first-time negotiator makes, it is invisible until the plan documents arrive after
signing, and by then the only remaining lever is the next review.
