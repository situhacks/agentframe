# Offer Terms — what to verify before accepting

Loaded at step 12, alongside [`comp-case.md`](deliverables/comp-case/template.md). The comp case argues
the **number**. This file covers **everything else the number is attached to**, because most of a package
is never discussed on the call and a candidate who asks only about base finds out what they agreed to
weeks later.

Run this before the offer call, not after it. Once the answer is yes, every question below becomes a
request for a favour.

## The distinction that decides where to spend the call

**Individually negotiable:** base, signing bonus, equity grant, vacation, title, start date, and
sometimes a one-time allowance.

**Not individually negotiable:** the group benefits plan, the retirement match formula, the disability
schedule. These are carrier contracts and plan rules covering every employee. Nobody gets a personal
drug coinsurance.

That split is the whole strategy. **A below-market benefit is not something to fix; it is an argument
for more base.** *"Your match is about 2% where market is 3 to 5 — that's roughly $3,400 a year. Can we
close that in base?"* is a specific, winnable ask. It is unavailable the moment the offer is accepted,
which is why the plan documents have to be read first.

## Request these before answering

Ask at the offer call, in one message, and expect them same-day. **"Full health, dental and extended
benefits" is not a disclosure.** Get the documents.

- The **group benefits summary** — the carrier's plan-at-a-glance, not a sentence in the offer letter
- The **retirement plan details** — match formula, vesting, provider
- The **full offer letter with every attachment**, including the equity plan and grant agreement
- The **vacation and leave policy** if the letter states only a number

Then take 24 to 48 hours. Asking for it is routine and reads as diligence, not hesitation.

## What to verify

**Cash.** Base. Bonus: target percent, how it is calculated, whether year one is prorated, and whether
an active-employment clause voids it if you leave before payout. Signing bonus: the clawback window, the
triggers, and **whether repayment is gross or net** — a gross clawback means repaying more than was
received.

**Equity.** Instrument, quantity, strike or grant price, current valuation and its date, vest schedule
and cliff, what happens at a liquidity event, and the **post-termination exercise window**, which is
where option value quietly dies.

**Retirement.** The match formula, stated as a **percent of salary** so it can be benchmarked — a flat
dollar cap looks generous until divided by the salary. Whether employer contributions vest. Whether the
plan consumes personal contribution room.

**Health and dental.** Coinsurance and annual maximum on drugs. Dental maximum, basic versus major
split, orthodontics. Paramedical: per-practitioner or one shared pool. Vision. Eligibility date and any
waiting period.

**Disability and life.** Long-term disability percentage, monthly cap, **elimination period**, and
whether the benefit is taxable — an employee-paid premium usually means tax-free benefits, which is the
better design. Whether short-term disability exists to bridge the elimination period, or whether state
or federal sickness benefits are expected to. Life insurance as a multiple of salary.

**Time.** Vacation days, accrued or front-loaded, carryover rules, payout on exit. Sick and personal
days. Parental leave top-up, if relevant.

**Work terms.** Required in-office days and whether that is contractual or policy. Travel expectation.
Probation length and what can happen during it. **Termination terms** — statutory minimum only, or
common-law notice preserved. Non-compete, non-solicit, IP assignment.

**Conditions.** What checks the offer is contingent on, and what can withdraw it.

## Calibrating "competitive"

Ranges move, so verify against current sources rather than trusting a remembered number. As orientation
for someone who has never seen a package priced:

| | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Employer retirement match | 3–5% of salary |
| Life insurance | 1–2× salary |
| Long-term disability | ~66.67% of earnings, monthly cap, 90–120 day elimination |
| Drug coinsurance | 80% is standard; 100% is a premium plan |
| Paramedical | per-practitioner limits are more common than one shared pool |
| Vacation | statutory minimum is a floor, not a market rate |

A single below-market line does not make a bad offer. **One light item inside an otherwise average plan
is normal; the failure is not knowing which item it was.**

## The failure this file exists to stop

Accepting a base in the belief that the rest of the package is competitive, because nobody asked. It is
the most common mistake a first-time negotiator makes, and it is invisible until the plan documents
arrive after signing. By then the only remaining lever is the next compensation review.
